Taste gets talked about like a gift, but it behaves like a muscle. Nobody is born knowing why one interface feels considered and another feels assembled. You develop the sense the same way you develop any sense — exposure, attention, repetition.

The word sounds precious, but the practice is mundane. Taste is mostly *noticing*. Why does this settings page feel calm? Why does that empty state feel abrupt? The people we describe as having great taste are usually just people who have asked those small questions thousands of times.

## Collect deliberately

I keep a folder of interfaces that made me feel something. Not trends — specifics. A modal whose entrance felt inevitable. A form that made errors feel recoverable instead of scolding. When I collect one, I write a sentence about *why* it works, because the why is the part that transfers.

A few habits that compound:

- Recreate one small interaction a week, from scratch, without looking.
- When something feels off, don't scroll past it — name the reason.
- Read fewer roundups; study more single products in depth.

## Instinct is cached judgment

Eventually the questions stop being conscious. You nudge the spacing before you can articulate why, the same way a musician hears a wrong note without checking the sheet. That's all instinct is — judgment you've run so many times it got cached.

Which means taste isn't a prerequisite for doing the work. It's the residue of doing it.
